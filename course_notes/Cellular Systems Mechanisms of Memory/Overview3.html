2Recurring principles

P1Thresholds come from binding requirements

  1. One signal rises (Ca²⁺ or cAMP).
  2. Its effectors differ in affinity and in how many ions or molecules they must bind.
  3. A small signal reaches only the most sensitive effector; more signal recruits the others.
  4. Which effectors run sets the outcome.

A Ca²⁺ or cAMP "threshold" is not a separate sensor: it is the binding requirement of whichever effector the signal reaches first.

P2A shared, limited pool makes repeated input add up sublinearly

  1. Several consumers draw on one limited pool.
  2. A strong or repeated signal ties up the pool.
  3. The next signal finds less free and produces a smaller increment.
  4. The output depends on spacing and on how the pool is refilled.

The temporal sensitivity of PKA comes from its upstream supply (AC1, CaM), not from PKA itself; a closed single compartment exaggerates this depletion, which is why both models add calmodulin back.

AnalogyMassed trains are like pumping a well before it has refilled. Breaks down: AC1 is not used up, only occupied, and CaM is refilled from outside the spine rather than by waiting alone.

P3The temporal pattern selects which branch carries the response

  1. The same total input arrives in different temporal patterns.
  2. Fast components summate within an event and decay; slow components need repeated or sustained input; depleted components need time to recover.
  3. One branch dominates.
  4. The requirement or the sign of plasticity changes.

Timing acts through time constants: what summates, what decays and what recovers between inputs. The intervals that matter sit near those time constants: milliseconds for NMDAR–spike coincidence, tens of seconds for cAMP and phosphoCaMKII.

P4Kinases feed back onto the control of their own signal

  1. A signal activates a kinase.
  2. The kinase phosphorylates the enzyme that removes the signal, or the phosphatase that erases its effect.
  3. Speeding removal = negative feedback (stabilises). Slowing removal or blocking the eraser = positive feedback (amplifies, prolongs).

Negative loops set how fast a signal returns to baseline; positive loops let it persist. Which wins depends on which kinase is faster: PKA early, ERK late.

AnalogyPKA on long PDE4 works like a thermostat: rising cAMP opens its own drain. Breaks down: a second, slower sensor (ERK) can partly close the same drain, so the setpoint drifts with time.

P5The readout chosen can decide the conclusion

  1. A quantity changes over time.
  2. It is summarised as a peak, a value at one time point, or an area or total.
  3. Different summaries can rank conditions differently.
  4. The right summary is the one the downstream mechanism actually integrates.

P6Neuromodulators gate which arms are available

  1. Activity supplies Ca²⁺.
  2. A neuromodulator acts on its own G-protein-coupled receptor.
  3. Both converge on one enzyme: AC1 via Gs, PLC via Gq.
  4. The arm runs fully only when both are present.

4Functional diagrams

Already drawn in the sources: role labels to add

Kim Fig 1A

pathway map · notes §2, §3
  • NMDAR = the Ca²⁺ source; Ca²⁺ amount codes activity
  • AC1 = coincidence detector of Ca²⁺/CaM and dopamine (Gs)
  • PDE1B = terminator recruited by the same Ca²⁺; PDE4 = constant terminator
  • I-1 = PKA's gate on PP1; PP2B = low-Ca²⁺ route that reopens the gate
  • PP1 = eraser of phosphoCaMKII; CaMKII = the massed-favouring kinase

Kim Fig 2C

interval curves · notes §3
  • Rising curve (PKA) = supply that improves with spacing
  • Falling curve (CaMKII) = summation lost with spacing
  • Flat filled circles = total kinase ≈ constant = compensation
  • Where CaMKII has settled low = the critical interval

Kim Fig 5C

fraction unbound · notes §3
  • Drop of the dotted (AC1) and dashed (CaM) lines between trains = how much of the pool is left for the next train
  • No drop when spaced = the pool has refilled

Paes Fig 1

PDE4 feedback scheme · notes §4
  • PKA → PDE4 long = fast negative loop (stabiliser)
  • ERK → PDE4 short = slow negative loop
  • ERK ⊣ PDE4 long and supershort = positive loop (amplifier)
  • Epac → Rap1 → ERK = slow second route from cAMP
  • 5′AMP = signal termination; the only enzymatic exit

Paes Fig 8B

share of degradation · notes §4
  • Long-form share falling while the short share grows = ERK taking over as it accumulates
  • Supershort share shrinking = the positive loop switching it off

Mäki-M. Fig 1

pathway map · notes §2, §5
  • Yellow (PKC) = low-threshold, Gq-gated LTD arm
  • Blue (PKA) = Gs-gated LTP arm
  • Green (CaMKII) = high-threshold LTP arm
  • Ng = CaM reservoir; PMCA/NCX = Ca²⁺ sink that sets how much reaches CaM

Mäki-M. Fig 2G–J and Fig 4D

thresholds and tetramers · notes §5
  • Order of the curves along the Ca²⁺ axis = threshold order PKC < PKA < CaMKII

D1The spine as a decoder of amount and context

Problem it solves: how one Ca²⁺ signal can produce either LTP or LTD.

Story: the amount of Ca²⁺ decides how many arms run, the modulators decide which arms may run, and the GluR1 fraction decides whether the combined receptor traffic reads out as LTP or LTD.

5Why questions

  1. Spaced trains give a lower PKA peak than massed trains, yet only spaced L-LTP needs PKA. Why, and why does the answer depend on what happens to AC1, calmodulin and CaMKII between trains?connect P2 · P3 · P5 · C2
  2. Why can a single cAMP pulse not tell PDE4 isoforms apart, while an oscillating input makes long isoforms dominant? What does this share with the spaced-versus-massed result?connect P3 · P4
  3. In the cortical model, PKC's job is to remove GluR2. Why does blocking the PKC pathway then shrink HFS-LTP, and why would the same block turn LFS-LTD into LTP?connect P1 · P6 · C2 · D1
  4. Why can one molecule, calmodulin, explain both the temporal sensitivity of PKA in the hippocampal model and the Ca²⁺ threshold order in the cortical spine?connect P1 · P2
  5. Two published models disagree on whether PKA activity depends on the inter-train interval. What would you check first, and why might neither model be wrong?connect P5 · C4 · D2

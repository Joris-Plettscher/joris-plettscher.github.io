1Overview map

Every topic answers one of three questions: how a memory is written and recalled (violet), how the hippocampus keeps room for the next one (green), and how neurons are made, kept alive and replaced (amber). Adult neurogenesis is the hub: it reruns the developmental program, renews memory capacity, and shows that repair by new neurons is possible.

Adult neurogenesis is development kept running in protected niches. Repair tries to restart development where the adult CNS has switched it off. That is why repair borrows developmental signals, and why the features that stabilize mature wiring are the same ones that block repair.

4Functional diagrams

Life of a memory trace in the hippocampus

Problem: keep a memory while freeing the hippocampus for the next one.

A trace is written fast and cleared in two speeds: sleep trims quickly, and the strong potentiation that survives is renormalized only as new neurons rewire the DG, by which time the memory depends on the neocortex instead.

How LTP raises neurogenesis in cells of different ages

Problem: LTP favors young neurons at several ages, although the youngest cannot yet be potentiated themselves.

LTP reaches young neurons by two routes: 2-week-old cells can be potentiated directly, while progenitors and 1-week-old cells, which lack glutamatergic input, benefit from the milieu that LTP in mature neighbours creates.

Stability vs renewal

Problem: circuits must stay stable to keep what was learned, yet stay changeable to learn more and to recover from injury.

The sources describe the same trade-off in two places: Ch. 57 says the stabilizers of mature wiring are why central axons regenerate poorly, and Alam shows that the DG keeps ongoing integration of new neurons; blocking that integration stabilizes old traces at the cost of new learning.

5Analogies

Analogy · capacity

The DG is a whiteboard. LTP writes on it; LTP saturation is a board covered edge to edge. LTD erases single words, sleep wipes off faint marks each night, and neurogenesis slowly swaps in fresh panels while the content has already been copied into a notebook (neocortex).

Where it breaks down: the new panels are not blank space only; new neurons also help write new memories and separate similar ones. And neurogenesis does not do the copying: cortex dependence grows alongside the clearing, it is not caused by it in these sources.

Analogy · NMDA receptor

The NMDA receptor is an AND gate: it passes current only when glutamate AND depolarization are present.

Where it breaks down: its output is graded, not on/off. A little Ca²⁺ gives LTD, a lot gives LTP, and spike order changes the amount.

6Why questions

  1. Running speeds recovery of contextual fear learning after LTP saturation, but irradiated runners do not recover faster. Why does this place neurogenesis between exercise and learning capacity, and where in that chain does LTP decay sit?
  2. Saturating LTP before training blocks new learning, while inducing LTP across the DG after training erases a memory. Why do both results support the same idea about how memories are stored?
  3. LTP raises the survival of 1-week-old granule cells that have no glutamatergic input, yet leaves the share of new cells that become neurons unchanged. Using the instructive/selective distinction and what neurotrophins do in development, why is this the expected pattern?
  4. Precursors that can make neurons exist in adult cortex and spinal cord, but neurogenesis normally happens only in the DG and SVZ, and injury-induced neurogenesis does not repair a stroke. What does this say about where the limit lies, and why might the features that keep adult wiring stable also stand in the way?
  5. A central neuron can die after its axon is cut far from the cell body. Why, and why does this make trophic support and cell replacement complementary strategies rather than alternatives?

02Recurring principles

Each template is stated once in plain words; the table maps the instances onto its stages.

P1 · Admit the new, mute the stored

Template
  1. Input arrives that must not be confused with what is already stored.
  2. ACh rises and cuts the pathway that would deliver stored patterns.
  3. The same rise opens the pathway that encodes or holds the new input.
  4. When ACh falls, the stored pathway returns and drives retrieval and consolidation.

The same gate appears at four grains — synapse type, rhythm phase, single cell, spatial metric — which is why ACh looks contradictory until you ask which pathway is being muted.

Analogy

A microphone and a monitor speaker in one room: raising the mic gain while muting the monitor lets the room be heard instead of the room's own echo.

Where it breaks down: the monitor's content here is not acoustic feedback but stored memory, and it is useful context rather than noise — exactly the disagreement in contrast table C3.

P2 · The brake is built into the signal

Template
  1. The signal acts.
  2. Acting engages a mechanism that shuts the same signal down.
  3. The signal is therefore short and self-limiting, which protects the target and sharpens timing.
P2 instances
InstanceActionShut-off it engagesWhat is protectedKey difference

Three brakes at three levels, and each one defines a different drug strategy: block the brake (esterase inhibitors), exploit the brake (self-limiting α7 agonism), or bypass it (allosteric modulation that needs endogenous ACh).

Analogy

α7 desensitisation as a circuit breaker: it trips on the current it is carrying.

Where it breaks down: a breaker is binary and has to be reset from outside, whereas desensitised receptors recover on their own and, while refractory, bind agonist with higher affinity than when resting.

P3 · Replace a lost signal while its targets still exist

Template
  1. A presynaptic source degenerates.
  2. The postsynaptic targets and their receptors are still there.
  3. Raising transmitter availability at those targets restores part of the function.
  4. When the targets themselves die, the same intervention has nothing left to act on.

P4 · Composition at the target, not the transmitter, sets the response

Template
  1. One transmitter arrives at many targets.
  2. Which subunits or subtype are assembled there decides sign, speed and calcium entry.
  3. The same release therefore produces different, sometimes opposite, effects at different targets.

P5 · A conserved site blocks selectivity, so go allosteric

Template
  1. The transmitter's own binding site is nearly identical across subtypes.
  2. A drug aimed at that site hits every subtype, including peripheral ones, so side effects cap the dose.
  3. Sites elsewhere on the receptor are far less conserved.
  4. A ligand there gains selectivity, and if it only modulates, it also follows endogenous activity instead of replacing it.

The shift from orthosteric to allosteric is the same move in both receptor families: stop imitating the transmitter and start changing how the receptor answers it.

04Functional diagrams

D1 · Who aims the broadcast

Problem it solves: ACh is released widely and slowly, so something outside the cholinergic system has to decide when the gain should go up — and something has to stop it going up without limit.

D2 · Holding an item that has no synapse to hold it

Problem it solves: a novel item has no strengthened synapses to fall back on, so the representation has to be held by the cell's own current — and only for items worth holding.

05Why questions

Why does ACh have to be destroyed in the cleft rather than recaptured, and what does that force about every drug designed to raise cholinergic transmission?Connects the life cycle to why there is no cholinergic equivalent of an SSRI.

Why does one rise in ACh make a cue easier to detect and, at the same time, make a similar stored pattern harder to retrieve?Requires the two halves of the core rule and the two receptor families that implement them.

Why does a receptor whose desensitisation protects the neuron also limit the drug strategy built on it, and why does chronic exposure change the receptor population rather than only its activation?Joins P2 with P4.

Why do cholinesterase inhibitors slow decline early in Alzheimer disease and lose effect later, when nothing about the drug's mechanism has changed?The expiry is a property of the circuit, not the molecule.

Why does schizophrenia research point at receptor subtypes while Alzheimer research points at a projection, even though both disorders present with impaired attention and memory?Compare where each lesion sits in the overview map.

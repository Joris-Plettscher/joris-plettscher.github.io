What the figures and the notes leave out: how the topics connect, which concepts are mistaken for each other, and what each mechanism is actually for.

01 — Overview map

One question, three strands

The spine is the causal chain from threat to disorder. The left column holds what makes and delivers the signal; the right column holds what modifies the chain and what it costs. Every edge states the relationship it carries.

The story: one hormone system is built to be switched on cheaply and off expensively; everything on the left decides how the signal arrives, everything on the right decides what it costs and where it breaks, and the two failure nodes at the bottom are the same switch read from opposite directions.

03 — Contrast tables

Pairs that get mistaken for each other

Each table lists only the dimensions on which the two actually differ.

MR vs GR

DimensionMRGR
Affinity and occupancyabout tenfold higher; substantially occupied already at the circadian troughlower; recruited at the circadian peak and by stress-induced rises
Distributionconcentrated in limbic regions — hippocampus, septum, amygdalain every cell; highest in PVN, hippocampus, amygdala, aminergic nuclei, PFC

The story: the same hormone is read twice — once to decide what to do, once to pay for stopping and remembering it.

Analogy A two-stage alarm: MR is the always-powered floor sensor that decides what to do about a signal; GR is the high-threshold system that shuts the alarm off and writes the incident report that the floor sensor will read next time. Where it breaks down: GR does not only terminate — it also supplies the energy for executive control; and MR is not a simple sensor, since it is nearly saturated, so its activity rather than the hormone level is the limiting factor.

Diagram A · The relay that sets the sign

Problem it solves The same cortex must be able to turn the axis down in one situation and up in another. The sign cannot live in the cortex, so it lives in the relay the cortex uses.

The story: physical stressors take the short road and arrive as they are, while every psychological signal is passed through a cell-type switch — which is why psychological stress is the modifiable kind, and why losing prefrontal input disinhibits the axis instead of silencing it.

Diagram B · Choosing a coping mode is choosing what it costs

Problem it solves An animal must act against a stressor it can control and conserve resources against one it cannot — and it must keep paying for the physiology of whichever it chose.

The story: two prefrontal subregions hold four output routes between them, graded from expensive and goal-directed to cheapest and automatic, and MR decides how far down that gradient the animal slides.

Diagram C · The episode loop

Problem it solves A stress episode has to leave the brain better prepared for the next one. The two receptors split that work and hand it back to each other across episodes.

The story: MR reads what GR wrote during the previous episode and GR writes what MR will read during the next, so resilience is a loop that closes rather than a single well-sized hormone response.

05 — Why questions

Five questions a list cannot answer

  1. Why does blocking MR lower vigilance and the sympathetic response while raising CORT output, when blocking GR does the opposite and prolongs the response?

    joins: MR/GR affinity split · peak vs duration · the relay and feedback topology

  2. Why can one chronic-stress trajectory end in hypercortisolaemic depression in one person and hypocortisolaemic PTSD in another, while both lose contextualization of the experience?

    joins: habituation of the daily peak · GR up- vs downregulation · hippocampal and mPFC atrophy against amygdala hypertrophy

  3. Why is an anti-glucocorticoid the right drug in the golden hours after trauma, and a glucocorticoid the right drug shortly before re-exposure — at the same receptor?

    joins: timing decides the sign · which memory operation is running · consolidation vs retrieval vs extinction

  4. Why is the proactive animal, whose HPA response looks better controlled, the less resilient one in a novel environment?

    joins: high limbic MR and the habit bias · energy economy · flexibility as the adaptive trait rather than a low hormone response

  5. Why does flattening the ultradian rhythm produce glucocorticoid resistance even when the amount of cortisol secreted in a day has not changed?

    joins: MR stays nuclear across pulses while GR binds and dissociates · hourly gene pulsing maintains responsiveness · receptor activity, not ligand level, as the limiting step

Dopamine: how one signal teaches, drives and misleads

A synthesis layer over the dopamine readings and the study notes: how the topics connect, which mechanisms recur, which concepts are easy to confuse, and what the circuits are for. Section numbers (§) point to the study notes.

Analogy (adaptive coding): a camera's auto-exposure stretches whatever scene it sees across the full brightness range. It breaks down because dopamine needs a cue announcing the range in advance, and its cue response still tracks expected value.

4Functional diagrams

Why a small setback outweighs a big win

Problem solved: keep predictions accurate and avoid what fails, at the price that wins are absorbed and misses are not.

Analogy: a high-jump bar raised after every clearance, so each success makes the next small miss more likely to count. It breaks down because the bar also drops after misses (the value baseline falls), and adaptive coding changes how sensitive the judge is, not only where the bar sits.

The depression cycle: when reinforcement stops steering behaviour

Problem the system normally solves: use wins and losses to adjust behaviour. In depression each half of that loop fails in a different way, and the failures feed each other.

5Why questions

  1. Why can a moderate dose of amphetamine ease negative symptoms while a high dose risks worsening them and triggering psychosis, although both raise dopamine?
  2. Why does a fully predicted reward evoke no burst in dopamine neurons, yet in Hamid's rats the [DA] peak at the reward cue stays at the same level across consecutive rewards?
  3. Why does the blunting of both prediction-error and value signals in schizophrenia point to a primary prediction-error deficit rather than a value deficit?
  4. Why do antipsychotics improve positive symptoms both quickly and progressively, and why can the same action make apathy worse?
  5. Why could the same small loss produce a larger dopamine dip in someone whose outcomes are usually consistent than in someone used to large swings, and why would its effect on motivation outlast the dip?
